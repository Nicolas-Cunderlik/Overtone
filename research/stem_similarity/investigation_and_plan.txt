STEM-BASED PERCEPTUAL SONG SIMILARITY - INVESTIGATION AND PLAN
Overtone research track, started 2026-10-19

======================================================================
1. MOTIVATION
======================================================================

Overtone recommends similar songs with a k-nearest-neighbor search over
Spotify's legacy audio features (danceability, energy, valence, tempo, ...)
for a static catalog of ~25.6k songs (TidyTuesday Spotify dataset, 2020).

Question: what does "similar" actually mean, and how do we measure it?

Working hypothesis: two songs sound similar when their component parts sound
alike - drums/percussion (the same clap sample makes two songs feel related),
bass, other instruments, vocals, and the overall "atmosphere" of the mix
(reverb, space, texture). Separate each song into stems, measure timbre
similarity per stem, and combine. Some stems will be more reliable or matter
more than others, so the combination weights must be learned, not assumed.

======================================================================
2. WHAT WE FOUND SO FAR
======================================================================

2.1 The current model and its yardstick

  - Features: 13 standardized Spotify audio features (key as sin/cos at
    0.25 weight), plus the 6 class probabilities of a genre classifier
    (HistGradientBoosting, weight 2). Cosine kNN.
  - Evaluation: genre precision@3 ~0.43, subgenre precision@3 ~0.18.
  - Problem: genre agreement is a weak stand-in for similarity. Six playlist
    genres say little about whether two songs actually sound alike.

2.2 Label proxies, tested and rejected

  Literature (Berenzweig/Ellis et al. ISMIR 2003; McFee et al. 2012;
  van den Oord et al. NeurIPS 2013) treats playlist / listening co-occurrence
  as the standard similarity ground truth. Our CSV has 471 playlists, so we
  scored candidate models on same-playlist precision@3 (chance ~0.4%):

    setup                               playlist@3 (song / unseen-artist split)
    audio features only                 4.1% / 3.8%
    + genre classifier (shipped)        4.3% / 3.9%
    learned per-feature diag weights    3.5% / 3.2%
    playlist-classifier embedding       5.0% / 4.6%
    playlist-classifier + release year  8.7% / 8.0%

  Takeaways: global per-feature weights don't help; release year (era) is the
  strongest metadata signal. But playlists, subgenres and genres all measure
  the context a song appeared in, not how it sounds. All three were rejected
  as the ground truth for this project.

2.3 Closest prior work

  Vohra & Akama, "Interpretable and Perceptually-Aligned Music Similarity
  with Pretrained Embeddings", arXiv:2601.19109 (Jan 2026):
    - Demucs v4 separation (4-stem htdemucs; 6-stem adds guitar/piano)
    - Frozen pretrained embeddings per stem (CLAP, MuQ-MuLan), cosine sim
    - Per-stem similarities combined linearly; weights fit on human ABX
      listening judgments (Inst-Sim-ABX, ~330 triplets, 281 listeners)
    - Agreement with humans: mix-only MuQ-MuLan 86.8% -> stem-weighted 90.4%
    - Learned weights (MuQ, 4-stem): drums 0.78, bass 0.25, vocals 0.08,
      other 0.86, full mix 1.94
    - 4-stem separation more robust; guitar/piano stems suffer bleed
  Limits we can address: their audio is MIDI-synthesized (Slakh) with no
  vocals, 5-second clips, and population-average weights.

2.4 Design decisions from the investigation

  - No raw waveform comparison. Identical sounds offset by milliseconds or
    mixed differently have ~zero waveform correlation. Compare learned
    embeddings / timbre descriptors instead.
  - No pitch or tempo normalization. Pretrained embeddings pooled over time
    are largely key/tempo tolerant. Pitch-shifting and time-stretching alter
    timbre (a kick shifted 5 semitones is a different kick) and depend on
    error-prone key/BPM estimates. Key and tempo stay as optional separate
    terms.
  - No single-note isolation. That's a transcription problem the embedding
    approach doesn't need. Phase-2 idea for the "same clap" case: detect
    individual hits in the drum stem and compare sets of hit embeddings.
  - "Atmosphere" is a property of the whole mix, so the full mix is kept as
    its own "stem" alongside drums / bass / vocals / other.
  - Audio source is the Deezer 30-second preview, never the audio playing on
    the user's device. Lookup by ISRC (Spotify track external_ids.isrc ->
    Deezer /track/isrc:...), or by artist + title search for catalog songs.

======================================================================
3. GROUND TRUTH: THE LISTENER'S OWN JUDGMENTS
======================================================================

[Superseded 2026-10-20: rating 400 triplets by hand was too slow. The ~20
ratings collected are kept in ratings.jsonl, but ground truth now comes from
public datasets - see section 9. The protocol below still describes how
triplet judgments are scored.]

With label proxies rejected, the remaining ground truth is human perception.
The listener (project author) rates triplets.

3.1 Primary task: ABX forced choice on the full mix

  Hear reference X and candidates A and B (same 10 s excerpt window of each
  preview, instant switching). Answer: "Which sounds more like X?"
  -> A / B / can't tell.

  Why forced choice instead of 1-10 ratings or free text:
    - Relative judgments are far more consistent than absolute scales, which
      drift within and between sessions.
    - Each answer is directly a training constraint:
      sim(X,A) > sim(X,B).
    - Same protocol as the prior work, so results are comparable.

  Why the full mix rather than stem pairs: the goal is to predict whole-song
  similarity. If the listener only rates drum stems, we learn whether the
  drum embedding matches drum perception, but not how much drums matter
  relative to bass or vocals overall. Stem weights are inferred from
  full-mix answers.

3.2 Secondary task: per-stem ABX (small subset)

  Same protocol on isolated stems (e.g. drums-only X/A/B). It measures
  whether each stem's embedding agrees with perception of that stem, i.e.
  stem reliability, separately from stem importance.

3.3 Feedback richness: structured tags + optional free text

  After each full-mix answer, optional one-click "because of" tags:
    drums/percussion | bass | vocals | instruments/melody |
    atmosphere/space | groove/tempo | era/production
  plus an optional free-text note.

  - Tags are cheap and directly analyzable: they say which stem drove a
    decision, an extra signal for the stem weights.
  - Free text is kept for qualitative analysis. MuQ-MuLan is a joint
    text-audio model, so notes like "airy reverb pads" can later be embedded
    and tested against the chosen song - a possible research angle.
  - Free text is optional, so rating stays fast. The fitting never depends
    on it.

3.4 Triplet selection (where the information is)

  Random triplets are mostly obvious or mostly noise. Pilot mix:
    50% stem-disagreement: A is X's nearest neighbor by one stem (e.g.
        drums), B by another (e.g. bass). The answer reveals which stem
        matters more. This is the most informative type.
    30% near vs far: A from X's top neighbors, B random. Sanity / anchor.
    20% random vs random: calibration, guards against selection bias.
  Plus ~10% repeats with A/B swapped, to measure self-consistency (the
  noise ceiling no model can beat).

  Target: ~300 judgments in sessions of ~50 (~25 s each, ~20 min/session).

======================================================================
4. PILOT (300 songs)
======================================================================

  Step 1  Sample 300 catalog songs, stratified across the 24 subgenres
          (diversity only - subgenre is not used as ground truth).
  Step 2  Fetch Deezer previews (artist + title search, duration and fuzzy
          title check). Cache MP3s locally, never redistributed.
  Step 3  Separate with Demucs htdemucs (4 stems). Reuse the checkpoint
          already shipped with Ultimate Vocal Remover.
  Step 4  Embed mix + drums + bass + vocals + other with MuQ-MuLan (frozen,
          512-d), on the 10 s rating excerpt and on the full 30 s. Also
          compute per-stem RMS energy (silence gating) and MFCC statistics
          (classic, interpretable timbre baseline).
  Step 5  Sanity checks: silent-stem rates, hubness (k-occurrence skew),
          listen to some neighbor lists.
  Step 6  Local rating app (localhost page, ratings to JSONL).
  Step 7  Fit and evaluate (section 5).

  Everything except Demucs and MuQ is already in the stack. Research code
  lives in its own venv (research/stem_similarity/.venv) so the app's
  environment stays light until we decide to ship.

======================================================================
5. EVALUATION
======================================================================

  Main metric: ABX agreement on held-out judgments (100 random 70/30
  splits, mean +- std), compared across:
    random (50%) | current Spotify-feature kNN | MFCC baseline |
    MuQ mix only | MuQ stems, equal weights | MuQ stems, fitted weights |
    fitted weights + release year/key/tempo terms
  Ceiling: listener self-consistency on repeated triplets.

  Weight fitting: same as prior work, a linear model on per-stem cosine
  differences, sim(X,A)-sim(X,B) per stem -> choice (logistic or ridge,
  non-negative weights). Bootstrap confidence intervals on each weight
  answer "which stems matter / are reliable".

  Secondary analyses:
    - per-stem ABX agreement (stem reliability vs importance)
    - tag frequencies vs fitted weights (do stated reasons match weights?)
    - do genre/playlist agreement predict the listener's choices?
      (quantifies how bad the label proxies really are)

======================================================================
6. KNOWN BIASES AND HOW WE HANDLE THEM
======================================================================

  Silent stems     No bass -> near-silent stem whose embedding matches all
                   other silent stems. Gate by RMS energy, drop that stem
                   term, renormalize the weights.
  Separation bleed "other" is a catch-all and leaks. Use 4-stem, not 6-stem.
                   Per-stem ABX measures the damage.
  Hubness          A few songs become everyone's neighbor in high-d cosine
                   space. Measure k-occurrence skew, apply mutual-proximity
                   normalization if needed.
  Excerpt choice   One 30 s preview (and a 10 s window of it) may not
                   represent the song. The model is evaluated on exactly what
                   the listener heard, and full-preview embeddings are
                   compared separately.
  Single listener  Weights are personal. That's a feature for the app, but a
                   limitation for general claims.
  Order effects    Randomize A/B order and triplet order. Repeats measure
                   drift.
  Codec            All audio is the same Deezer preview codec. Uniform,
                   which is good.

======================================================================
7. PUBLISHING / SHOWING IT OFF
======================================================================

  Realistic first venue: an ISMIR Late-Breaking/Demo extended abstract
  (short, low bar, demo-friendly), plus a write-up and an open repo.

  Possible contributions over prior work:
    1. Stem-weighted similarity on real commercial music, vocals included
       (the prior work used synthetic, vocal-free audio).
    2. Personalized vs population stem weights.
    3. A direct measurement of how poorly genre / playlist / Spotify
       audio-feature similarity predicts perceived similarity.
    4. Free-text listener notes vs text-audio embeddings.

  To strengthen beyond a demo: recruit 5-10 additional raters (same app).
  That gives inter-rater agreement and population weights, and turns n=1
  into a real study.

  Reproducibility: previews can't be redistributed. Release Deezer/Spotify
  track IDs, ratings, embeddings and code. Check the model licenses (MuQ-MuLan
  weights are believed to be non-commercial; fine for research, verify
  before shipping in the app).

======================================================================
8. LATER: SHIPPING IN THE APP
======================================================================

  New song -> Spotify ISRC -> Deezer preview -> Demucs -> MuQ stem
  embeddings -> weighted kNN over precomputed catalog stem embeddings.
  Few seconds on GPU (RTX 3070), tens of seconds on CPU. FreqBlog audio
  features become a supporting signal (tempo/key/era terms) instead of the
  primary one. Full-catalog embedding is a one-off batch job (~a day of GPU
  time, to be measured in the pilot).

======================================================================
9. PIPELINE V2: DATASET-DRIVEN GROUND TRUTH (2026-10-20)
======================================================================

9.1 Principle: signals are inputs, people are targets

  Stem embeddings, MFCC timbre, Spotify features, genre probabilities and
  release year are all model INPUTS. Their weights are fit to targets that
  come from people. Mixing a model input into the label would grade the
  model on agreeing with itself. Each target is reported separately, never
  averaged into one number.

9.2 Targets

  T0 Perceptual, expert - the author's own ABX ratings (listener_abx adapter,
     ratings.jsonl): a music producer judging sound selection, with per-stem
     ratings (facet = drums/bass/vocals/other), reason tags and notes. Small
     (19 so far) but the most specific signal; the held-out check that
     matters most. Grows with short rating sessions on informative triplets.
  T1a Perceptual - DIM-SIM (Lee et al. ICASSP 2020, zenodo 3889149): direct
     "is song 1 or 2 closer to the anchor?" votes - 3,781 untied triplets,
     39,440 votes (5-12 raters each), 879 flagged clean (>= 90% agreement).
     MSD ids -> artist/title via MSD unique_tracks.txt -> Deezer previews.
     Caveat: votes were on specific 3 s moments of 7digital previews (start
     times kept in meta); Deezer previews cover other moments - song-level
     evidence. CC BY-NC 4.0.
  T1 Perceptual - MagnaTagATune "odd one out" (TagATune bonus game):
     346 unique triplets, ~7,650 votes, 1,019 clips (audio: 3 x 1 GB zip,
     32 kbps MP3). Majority outlier c of (a, b, c) gives two constraints:
     sim(a,b) > sim(a,c) and sim(a,b) > sim(b,c), weighted by vote margin.
     Caveats: low bitrate hurts separation; agreement is often weak.
  T2 Behavioral - Last.fm track.getSimilar, fetched live per seed track
     (LASTFM_API_KEY in .env). The match score gives graded relevance, so the
     model's neighbor ranking is scored with nDCG@k / AUC. It measures what
     people listen to together, not purely what sounds alike.
  T3 (optional) Perceptual pairs - CCMSim (github.com/pxaris/CCMSim): 1,130
     pairs rated for overall similarity, about 1 rating each, mostly
     non-Western. Use the MagnaTagATune/FMA (Western) subset, scored with
     Spearman correlation.
  Checked, not usable (2026-10-20):
     "MuSHeRD" - no such dataset found in the literature.
     Spotify Music Streaming Sessions (skips) - track ids are anonymized and
       carry no artist/title, so no audio can be fetched; challenge closed.
     Inst-Sim-ABX (Hashizume et al., per-instrument ABX, 586 listeners) -
       ideal for stem facets but not publicly released; worth asking the
       authors.
     MATCHA (attribute-level triplets) - no public release found.
  Not used: AIME (disco-eth/AIME, 62 GB). Its 15,600 human judgments are
     pairwise music-quality and text-audio-alignment preferences between
     mostly AI-generated tracks. No reference song, so no similarity signal.
  Controlled checks: the author's one-shot sample library (kicks, basses...)
     or NSynth / FSD50K. Stem embeddings should group same-type sounds, and a
     sample planted in two different loops should link them on that stem.

9.3 Stages - each a separate, resumable process

  (The plan as written on 2026-10-20. The built stages differ - e.g. stems
  are FLAC, there are no timbre/targets stages, and an isrc stage runs before
  audio. CLAUDE.md in this folder and the stemsim module docstrings describe
  the pipeline as it is.)

  All stages share one registry (SQLite, WAL mode: concurrent readers plus
  one writer per stage). Each stage processes only rows that are ready
  (inputs exist) and not yet done (its output is missing). It writes its
  output, marks the row, and can be killed or restarted at any time. No
  stage waits for another to finish everything, so network, GPU and CPU
  work overlap, and evaluation runs on whatever subset is complete.

  stage            resource          input -> output
  S1 ingest        CPU, once         TidyTuesday catalog + MTAT metadata -> registry rows
  S2 lastfm        network ~4 req/s  seed tracks -> similar lists (raw JSON per seed);
                                     new similar tracks registered (depth 1, capped)
  S3 audio         network 50 req/5s tracks without audio -> Deezer 30 s preview
                                     (MTAT: extracted from its zip instead)
  S4 separate      GPU               audio -> 4 stems + mix, excerpt windows (OGG)
  S5 embed         GPU               stems -> MuQ-MuLan (later CLAP) vectors, keyed by
                                     (model, window) - adding a model never re-separates
  S5b timbre       CPU               stems -> MFCC / RMS / onset stats, silence flags
  S6 targets       CPU               T1/T2/T3 -> triplets/pairs among embedded tracks
  S7 fit/evaluate  CPU               targets + vectors -> stem weights, per-target report,
                                     runs logged to MLflow (local)

  Rate limits, retries and caches live inside each stage. A Last.fm or Deezer
  outage stalls only that stage, never the GPU.

9.4 Budget (measured on the first ~1-2k items, 2026-10-20)

  Last.fm raw   16.5 KB/seed x 25.6k                 ~0.4 GB, ~2.5 h
                (31% of seeds get a list; similar songs outside the catalog
                are NOT given audio - that would be ~150k songs / ~70 GB)
                Decided 2026-10-28 (SSM-31): 106,750 Last.fm-only songs stay
                without audio; isrc/audio skip them unless run with
                --dataset lastfm (config.NO_AUDIO_DATASETS). T2 doesn't need
                them: 120k of its 392k pairs already have audio on both
                sides (92k embedded). Revisit with a capped sample if T2
                scoring runs short.
  Deezer        467 KB/preview x ~24k catalog songs  ~11 GB, ~6 h
  MTAT          3.3 GB archive (+0.1 GB extracted clips; archive deletable)
  Stems         ~720 KB per song per 10 s window (5 OGGs) - kept only for
                rated / perceptual-eval tracks (~1-2k songs, ~1.5 GB); catalog
                songs keep embeddings only (would be ~18 GB as stems)
  Embeddings    ~20 KB/song (5 stems x 2 windows x 512 floats)  ~0.5 GB
  Total         ~16 GB on C: (research/stem_similarity/data, gitignored)

9.5 Data quality

  Deezer search can return another version of a song (a different remix, the
  live or acoustic cut). The pilot's fuzzy matching accepted ~20 of 300 such
  mismatches; stages/audio.same_recording now requires version words (remix,
  live, acoustic, extended...) to match exactly. listener_abx keeps the
  pilot's audio regardless - the ratings judged what was actually heard.

  That rule also rejected real matches with extra title words, leaving 7,306
  tracks unavailable (SSM-26). Audio is now looked up by ISRC first (found on
  Spotify by stages/isrc.py), with title search as the fallback:
    pilot (25 differing titles)   ISRC right for 25/25
    200-song sample               69% recovered; 1 ISRC data error, rejected
    re-run, spotify_catalog       +3,375 (coverage 81% -> 94%)
    re-run, dim_sim               +872 (78% -> 86%); 1,412 have no Spotify
                                  match by name, the main remaining gap
  Extra plain title words are accepted only within 2 s of the duration
  (pilot: +2 right, 0 wrong; 12 s let in an acoustic cut).
